# CHROME — breakdown

## The hook

A beauty house in Seongsu-dong refuses to call its lip glosses a color. Every
pour is a metal — Liquid Mercury, Iridescent Pearl, Gunmetal Veil, Hot
Hologram — and the page's only job is to make you believe the metal is wet.
The headline is not chrome-*colored* type, it is a fragment shader pouring
liquid metal into the glyph shapes; the buttons are wet chrome bands; the
cards are sealed in three offset rainbow foil layers. It is 2003's conviction
that everything looks better chrome, rendered as pure code.

The design bet is the family line. This entry shares its aesthetic family
(retro-y2k) with PIXEL — but PIXEL owns pixel-art arcade: blocky, cheerful,
a CRT grid of discrete cells. CHROME owns liquid chrome beauty: smooth,
metallic, reflective, continuous gradients, the opposite of a grid. A
grayscale screenshot of each is unmistakable. PIXEL is a mosaic of squares;
CHROME is a smooth gradient with a moving specular. The two share only the
decade.

## Art direction

Chrome-on-light is the trickiest brief in the gallery, because the obvious
move — set copy in shiny silver or holographic pink — fails contrast
immediately. Chrome silver `#c8d0d8` measures 1.40:1 as text on the
holo-mist background; the bright accent pink `#ff8ad8` is worse at 1.91:1.
So the discipline is absolute: **chrome silver and accent pink are fills
only, never text.** Every functional text token runs on the deep chrome
shadow: chrome-ink `#2a2a3a` at 12.66:1 (AAA), chrome-ink-2 `#4a4a5e` at
7.76:1 (AA+), ink-soft `#6a6a7e` at 4.75:1 (AA). The bright metal is the
surface; the dark metal is the ink. The one place an accent color carries
text is the eyebrow / large-display register, where a deepened
`accent-deep #c84aa0` clears AA-large (3.82:1) — documented, never body.

The three faces are three registers. Syne is the Latin chrome display — its
800 weight fills like poured metal, and heads go tight (-0.02em) so the heavy
mass reads as one shape, not six letters. Black Han Sans is every Hangul
glyph, a chunky Y2K Korean display face that sounds like the lettering on a
flip-phone casing. Major Mono Display is the chrome console — the spec codes
(CRM-01…), the eyebrows, the footer legal — its even advance width is the
rhythm of a product sheet stamped into foil. Display stacks put Hangul second
so Korean typed in a heading falls through Syne into Black Han Sans with no
markup.

## Technique 1 — the chrome shader (liquid metal, with a fallback)

The signature is the headline: a WebGL fragment shader painting liquid metal
into the wordmark's glyph shapes. A single full-quad program, a horizontal
chrome band stack displaced by layered sine ripples and a gaussian lens that
follows the pointer:

```glsl
// the chrome: a 5-stop silver band stack, shadow edge -> silver -> highlight
vec3 chrome(float v) {
  vec3 a = mix(SHADOW, SILVER, smoothstep(0.00, 0.28, v));
  vec3 b = mix(a,       HILITE, smoothstep(0.28, 0.46, v));
  vec3 c = mix(b,       SILVER, smoothstep(0.46, 0.74, v));
  return   mix(c,       SHADOW, smoothstep(0.74, 1.00, v));
}
// the gradient coordinate is displaced by summed sines + a pointer gaussian
float lens = exp(-(dx * dx * 3.5 + dy * dy * 6.0));
g += lens * 0.10 * uPtr.z;   // the metal leans where you look
```

The palette is fixed to the entry's chrome tokens (shadow `#2a2a3a`, silver
`#c8d0d8`, highlight `#ffffff`) so the shader is a literal token render —
WebGL cannot sample CSS custom properties, so the stops are documented
literals that mirror `tokens.json`. The pointer drives a gaussian lens that
raises ripple amplitude where you look, lerped at 0.09/frame (energy attack
0.06, release 0.025 — notices slowly, forgets slower). On touch, or after
2.4s of pointer silence, an autonomous lissajous ripple keeps the metal
alive.

The honest part is the fallback. A shader alone is fragile: WebGL can be
absent, the context can be lost, the program can fail to compile or link. So
the wordmark is built in three layers:

1. a **CSS chrome-gradient text fill** (`background-clip: text`, a 5-stop
   ink→silver→highlight→silver→ink gradient) — always on, the no-JS and
   reduced-motion and failure state;
2. the **WebGL canvas** layered over it in `mix-blend-mode: screen`,
   transparent until its first real frame (`.is-live`), hidden entirely on
   any failure;
3. the **per-glyph sweep** (next section) that masks the whole stack.

If WebGL fails, the page loses the ripple — never the wordmark. The CSS fill
is the chrome, the shader is the wetness.

The loop is throttled to ~64fps, `dt` clamped to 48ms, DPR capped at 2, and
pauses offscreen (IntersectionObserver, 120px margin) and on
`visibilitychange`. Under reduced motion, one composed still frame is drawn
at a hand-picked `t = 4200` and the loop never starts.

## Technique 2 — holographic foil (pointer parallax of the rainbow sheen)

Every foil panel — the manifesto plaque, the product cards, the wordmark's
frame — is the same recipe: a chrome-silver base with three offset conic-
gradient bands (sky → pink → mint) blended in `mix-blend-mode: screen`, plus
a moving white specular streak. The rainbow sheen.

```css
.chrome-holofoil__band--pink {
  background: conic-gradient(
    from calc(120deg + calc(var(--shift-x) * -0.6)),
    var(--chrome-glow-pink), transparent 35%, var(--chrome-holo-pink) 60%, ...);
  filter: blur(14px);
  mix-blend-mode: screen;
}
```

The three bands' angular origins shift with `--chrome-px` / `--chrome-py`,
the pointer offset from the panel center (range -1..1), set by a
`useFoilParallax` rAF hook that lerps at 0.06/frame and caps the value. Each
band reacts at a different rate (±18°/±11°/±6° and opposite signs) so the
sheen never moves as one flat sheet — it reads as light catching a curved
foil. The white specular streak slides its `background-position` with the
same pointer source, the thing that makes the metal read as *wet* rather
than printed.

This is the honest basis for the `pointer-parallax` tag: three real layers
(sky, pink, mint) drifting with the pointer, lerped and capped, over a
fourth (the silver base). No transform-on-hover trickery — the layers
physically shift their gradient origins. On touch and under reduced motion
the foils rest at `--chrome-px: 0`, their neutral rainbow state; the chrome
shader carries the motion instead, so nothing is hover-only.

## Technique 3 — the metallic sweep (character-split reveal)

The wordmark arrives as a sweep, not a fade. Six aria-hidden glyph spans sit
behind an `aria-label`, each clipping open left-to-right as the metal crosses
it:

```css
.chrome-js .chrome-wordmark__ch {
  clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0);             /* start: clipped to nothing */
  transition: clip-path 620ms cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1);
  transition-delay: calc(var(--chrome-ci, 0) * 55ms);
}
.chrome-js .chrome-wordmark.is-visible .chrome-wordmark__ch {
  clip-path: inset(0 0 0 0);                 /* end: fully open */
}
```

`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — `ease-chrome` — is the metallic sweep: a
fast gather and a long, bright tail. The 55ms stagger means the sweep crosses
the wordmark like light travelling across metal. The glyphs themselves are
*transparent ink*: they exist only to define the headline's metrics and act
as a clip mask. The visible metal is the CSS fill + the WebGL shader layered
beneath them, revealed left-to-right as each glyph unclips. Because the mask
is `clip-path` (compositor-friendly) and the underlying metal is already
onscreen, the sweep costs nothing but six clip transitions.

The CSS default is fully unclipped, so without JavaScript and under reduced
motion the headline simply rests — the glyphs are open, the CSS chrome
gradient shows through, and the wordmark is complete and legible. The split
is real (`char-split-reveal`): an `aria-label` on the h1, `aria-hidden` on
the animated spans, per-glyph animation.

## Motion math

- **Shader pointer lerp 0.09 / energy attack 0.06 / release 0.025.** The
  chrome takes ~110ms to slew toward your hand, ~190ms half-life to brighten,
  ~480ms to forget you. Asymmetry is the personality — heavy liquid attention.
- **Foil lerp 0.06/frame.** Half-life ≈ 11 frames ≈ 180ms. The rainbow drifts
  like foil held in the hand, never snapping.
- **Ambient ripple 0.18 / 0.27 rad/s** on the two shader axes; idle lissajous
  runs three unrelated frequencies (0.00021 / 0.00033 / 0.00045); glow and
  hint pulse at 4.2s — no shared periods, nothing visibly loops in sync.
- **Char sweep 620ms, 55ms stagger.** Six glyphs cross in ~900ms total — long
  enough to read as a sweep, short enough not to test patience.

## Performance notes

- One rAF loop per animated system (shader, foil, reveal), each throttled to
  ~64fps (`FRAME_MS = 15.5`), `dt` clamped to 48ms. All three pause offscreen
  via IntersectionObserver (120px margin) *and* on `visibilitychange`.
- The WebGL shader is one full-quad draw call per frame — six vertices, a
  handful of uniforms. DPR capped at 2; the backing store tops out around
  2200×600 device pixels for the hero headline.
- The foil parallax writes two CSS custom properties per frame on one element;
  CSS does the gradient-origin math. No per-frame React re-renders anywhere —
  the reveal observer writes classes imperatively.
- Everything CSS-animates is `transform`, `opacity`, `filter`, or
  `clip-path`; the one exception is the foil specular streak's
  `background-position` (GPU-composited, far cheaper than repainting the conic
  bands).

## Accessibility notes

- **Reduced motion:** `usePrefersReducedMotion` (useSyncExternalStore over
  matchMedia) makes the shader render one composed still frame at `t = 4200`
  with no loop and no listeners; the glyphs rest fully unclipped; the foil
  rests at neutral rainbow; a media query removes every reveal transition and
  the glow/hint pulses. The page becomes a calm, fully-legible chrome poster.
- **No-JS:** every pre-reveal state is gated behind `.chrome-js`, added on
  mount. The CSS chrome-gradient fill is the headline's default, so the
  wordmark's no-JS state is the finished one; the holo scrim backs the canvas
  so there is never a hole; all copy, cards, and the creed are plain DOM.
- **Semantics:** the wordmark is an h1 with `aria-label="CHROME — 크롬"` over
  aria-hidden glyph spans; the shader canvas is `role="img"` with a bilingual
  description; the chrome pills are `role="img"` with bilingual labels; the
  foil layers are decorative and hidden.
- **Contrast:** chrome-ink 12.66:1, chrome-ink-2 7.76:1, ink-soft 4.75:1 on
  holo-mist — all functional text AA or better. Chrome-silver (1.40:1) and
  accent pink (1.91:1) never carry text; accent-deep (3.82:1, AA-large)
  carries accent-colored text only at eyebrow/large-display scale.

## Remix ideas

1. **Chrome energy drink.** Keep the shader headline and foil cards; redraw
   the chrome pills as cans, retitle the line by caffeine content. The
   holographic underglow becomes the can's refrigerated condensation.
2. **Car paint showroom.** The shader stays chrome but the foil shifts to
   automotive candy flake; the product grid becomes paint chips you can tilt
   (pointer parallax on each chip's flake orientation).
3. **Skateboard deck shop.** Invert the gloss: the chrome becomes the trucks
   and hardware, the foil becomes the deck art. The sweep reveal lands on each
   deck's foil-stamped logo.
