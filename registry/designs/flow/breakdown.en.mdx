# FLOW — breakdown

## The hook

A journaling app whose whole identity is that *letters flow like water*. The
page's signature is a canvas river: thirty words — 새벽, 비, 창문, 종이, 잉크,
강, 바다 and the seasons, hours, and sensations a journal actually holds —
drift across the frame along sine waves, in three planes of depth. The far
plane is small, faint, and slow; the near plane is large, crisp, and fast.
A pointer parts them like a stone in the current — a soft radial nudge that
lerps back to rest when the hand leaves. This is the same three-depth
composition the dream entry uses for its clouds, restated for type.

A pinned verse section holds while four stanzas flow past with the scroll.
Each stanza owns a quarter of the scroll; they crossfade rather than cut, so
the verses read as one river, four readings. And a feature section names
five real laws of liquid — rheology, surface tension, laminar flow,
turbulence, viscosity — with their real constants and the Reynolds-number
threshold where flow turns turbulent, and reads each as a law of writing.

## Techniques

1. **canvas-particles** — the WordRiver. A DPR-capped canvas draws a few
   dozen words along sine waves in three depth bands. The simulation is a
   pure function of (state, dt): phases advance, x wraps, the pointer push
   lerps back to the sine baseline. NaN discipline carried over from pulse
   — every measured value is guarded with `Number.isFinite`.
2. **char-split-reveal** — the hero wordmark drifts in glyph by glyph, each
   letter settling sideways from a skew.
3. **scroll-scrub-pinned** — the verse section. A 400vh outer section, a
   sticky inner stage, one `--flow-scrub` value (0→1) drives every stanza's
   dissolve via pure `calc()`/`max()`/`clamp()` arithmetic.
4. **pointer-parallax** — the hero copy leans gently toward the cursor at
   three depths: the kicker barely moves, the title carries the drift, the
   nearest glyphs read at arm's length.

## Accessibility

Reduced motion: the canvas composes a single still frame at its seed
composition; the wordmark does not drift in; the pointer parallax never
writes its variables; the verses show their last stanza ("all into the same
sea"). No-JS: the full page is readable — the river replaced by the CSS sky
wash, the verses stacked, the laws a static list. AA contrast on every body
token; the bright cyans are decorative (large/glyph) only. Korean-first
bilingual throughout, with `:lang(ko)` line-height/letter-spacing rules.

## Why canvas, not CSS gradients

The first version of this entry tried to fake a river with a
`repeating-linear-gradient` of diagonal stripes. It read as static stripes,
not flowing water, and the "flowing verse" was seven words in a flex row.
The canvas river fixes both: the words actually move along sines, the
pointer actually pushes them, and the verse section carries four full
stanzas. The water is drawn in code, every frame.
