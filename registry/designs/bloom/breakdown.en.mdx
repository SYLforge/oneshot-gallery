# 피다 BLOOM — breakdown

## The hook

There is a perfume house in Seogwipo that does not sell you a bottle. It
sells you *the act of opening* — the half-hour during which a scent
unfolds from its bright top through its floral heart to its root base.
The page is that half-hour, rehearsed. You arrive at a warm-cream sheet
of paper, and an ink-line botanical draws itself stem-first as you
settle, the way a perfumer's plate would fill in a 19th-century scent
journal. A blush of watercolor blooms outward behind the lines and finds
its edge — *ink on wet paper*, the namesake behavior — and then one
pinned flower opens petal-by-petal with your scroll, the house scent
literally unfurling.

The design bet: on a light page where the only imagery is hand-drawn
SVG, *a pigment spreading and settling* can carry more atmosphere than
any photograph — because a photograph of a bloom is a record of a thing
that already happened, and this page is a thing happening.

## Art direction — and the load-bearing distinction from SAKURA

This is the second of three `ink-bloom` entries, and the family thesis
(*ink → bloom*) is honored by both — but with deliberately different
instruments, so a grayscale screenshot of each is unmistakable. SAKURA
(the first) is a dark canvas scattered with cherry-blossom particles:
night, falling petals, pink on black. BLOOM is a warm-cream sheet where
SVG line-art draws itself and watercolor blooms behind it: daylight, a
perfumer's plate, green and blush on paper. Where SAKURA's bloom is a
*thing moving* (petals drift and fall), BLOOM's bloom is a *behavior on
paper* (pigment spreads and settles). Same family; opposite time of day,
different medium.

The palette enforces it. The ground is warm cream `#faf3e8` (paper, never
white), the voice is ink `#2a2620` (12.4:1, the color of the drawing pen
itself), and there is exactly one accent — botanical green `#3d6b3a`
(6.9:1) — which appears wherever a line asks for attention: the
wordmark, the section numerals, the CTA, the focus ring, the deepest
stems. Sage `#8aa67a` and blush `#d98a9e` are the two prettiest colors
on the page and they both fail AA (2.4:1 and 1.9:1), so they are
pigment, never a voice: leaf fills and the single petal wash. Secondary
text is a derived token instead, `bark #5c554a` (5.7:1).

Two typefaces, two hands. Cormorant Garamond is the perfumer's Latin
hand — the wordmark, the section titles, every italic aside (the lines
written in the margin of the formula). Noto Serif KR is the ground: all
Hangul, the first-class script of the page. The display stack `Cormorant
Garamond, Noto Serif KR, serif` means the giant 피다 falls through the
Latin face into Noto Serif KR with no markup — the title's Hangul weight
is a font-stack accident made load-bearing, exactly as sup does with
Gowun Dodum but to the opposite effect (a serif brush, not a round sans).

## Technique 1 — a botanical that draws itself (svg-line-draw)

The hero plant is a hand-authored SVG plate — a flowering stem with two
branches, four leaves, a calyx, and a five-petal bud. Unlike sup (which
grows a procedural L-system), bloom's line-art is literal path data,
authored once at module load and identical on server and client —
hydration diffs are structurally impossible.

The interesting part is the drawing clock. Every stroke carries its own
window `[d0, d1]` on a shared 0→1 clock, recording *when on the
narrative it is drawn*: the stem is `0.00–0.18`, the branches
`0.18–0.34`, the leaves `0.30–0.58`, the bud-and-flower `0.58–1.00`.
Every path gets `pathLength={1}` so dash math ignores real length, and
then one CSS variable draws the whole plate:

```css
/* styles.css — one variable, ~40 private stroke timelines */
.bloom-draw__stroke {
  stroke-dasharray: 1 1;
  stroke-dashoffset: clamp(
    0,
    calc((var(--d1, 1) - var(--bloom-draw, 1)) /
         (var(--d1, 1) - var(--d0, 0))),
    1.02
  );
}
```

When `--bloom-draw` is 0, every offset is `(d1 − 0)/(d1 − d0)` — a big
positive number, clamped to 1.02, meaning the dash is fully offset and
the stroke is invisible. As the clock climbs, each stroke's offset
crosses zero in its own window and the stroke appears to *draw* from
its start point. A rAF hook (`useLineDraw`) eases the variable to 0.7
over ~2.4s and lets scroll raise the last 30%:

```ts
// useLineDraw.ts — intro + scroll, lerped so it never snaps
const intro = easeOut(Math.min((now - born) / INTRO_MS, 1)) * INTRO_REACH;
const scroll = Math.min(window.scrollY / (window.innerHeight * SCROLL_SPAN), 1);
const target = Math.min(1, intro + (1 - INTRO_REACH) * scroll);
const next = value + (target - value) * DRAW_LERP * (dt / 16.7);
```

Three details carry the a11y story. First, the fallback is
`var(--bloom-draw, 1)`, so no-JS and reduced-motion visitors see a
finished plate, never a bare stem. Second, the hidden-state offset clamps
at 1.02, not 1.0, so round line-caps can't leave a phantom dot at the
stroke base. Third, the same hook instance is reused per-card in the
scent-notes section — each plate draws independently as you scroll into
it, like three separate illustrations in a journal rather than one
synchronized animation.

## Technique 2 — ink finding its edge (feturbulence-texture)

This is the page's namesake behavior and the one that distinguishes
BLOOM's *bloom* from SAKURA's. SAKURA blooms by *scattering particles* —
a thing moving. BLOOM blooms by *spreading pigment* — a behavior on
paper. The mechanism is an SVG `feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap` filter
on each watercolor wash shape, whose `scale` attribute animates from
~62 down to 0 as the wash enters view:

```ts
// useWashSettle.ts — the wash begins "still wet" and settles
const settle = (svg) => {
  const fe = maps.get(svg);
  const t0 = performance.now();
  const tick = (now) => {
    const p = Math.min((now - t0) / SETTLE_MS, 1);
    const ease = 1 - (1 - p) ** 3;        // cubic out — a slow bloom
    fe.setAttribute("scale", String(WET_SCALE * (1 - ease)));
    if (p < 1) rafs.add(requestAnimationFrame(tick));
  };
  rafs.add(requestAnimationFrame(tick));
};
```

At `scale=62`, the fractal noise displaces each pixel of the wash by up
to 62 units in a direction determined by the noise's R/G channels — the
color is smeared into the noise field, its edge dissolved, reading as
*still wet, still moving*. As the scale eases to 0, the displacement
collapses and the wash settles into its final crisp-ish edge — *the ink
found its boundary*. Crucially, only the `scale` animates; the
turbulence `baseFrequency` and `seed` never change, so the noise texture
is computed once by the browser and each frame pays only the
displacement pass. A separate rAF runs per wash, all IntersectionObserver
-gated, so offscreen cost is zero.

The central craft decision lives next to this filter: the botanical
**line-art** (stems, leaves, petals) is plain SVG strokes with *no*
displacement filter. Only the wash beneath the lines bleeds; the drawn
line stays crisp. That contrast — crisp line, blurred wash — *is* the
medium of watercolor, reproduced with two SVG features and one rAF loop.

A second, *static* `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.9, seed 7) sits fixed
over the whole page at ~4% cream-on-cream via `mix-blend-mode: multiply`
— the paper fiber. It never animates; it just keeps the cream from
reading as flat `#fff`. Distinguish from kemuri's washi grain (ink-dark
noise over a warm sheet): BLOOM's fiber is cream-on-cream, lighter, a
paper not a fabric.

## Technique 3 — the flower that opens with you (scroll-scrub-pinned)

The signature moment. A `position: sticky` section 260vh tall holds a
single inner viewport pinned while your scroll progress through the
section drives `--bloom-open` from 0 to 1. One large flower — 8 outer
petals, 5 inner, a gold disc, six anther dots — stays fixed in frame
while a rAF hook (`useScrollProgress`) lerps the variable toward the
scroll target:

```ts
// useScrollProgress.ts — the pinned section's scroll maps to one variable
const computeTarget = () => {
  const rect = el.getBoundingClientRect();
  const total = rect.height - window.innerHeight;
  if (total <= 0) { target = 1; return; }
  const traveled = Math.min(Math.max(-rect.top, 0), total);
  target = traveled / total;
};
```

Then CSS converts that one variable into every petal's transform. The
outer ring opens first, the inner ring opens later, so the flower reads
as unfurling outward-in — the way a real peony opens:

```css
/* styles.css — outer ring opens across 0.0–0.8 of the clock */
.bloom-sig-ring--outer .bloom-sig-petal {
  --bloom-local: clamp(0,
    calc((var(--bloom-open, 1) - var(--bloom-pstart) * 0.5) / 0.8), 1);
  transform-origin: 300px 300px;
  transform: rotate(calc(-8deg * (1 - var(--bloom-local))))
             scale(calc(0.42 + 0.58 * var(--bloom-local)));
  opacity: calc(0.4 + 0.6 * var(--bloom-local));
}
/* inner ring opens across 0.35–0.95 — later */
.bloom-sig-ring--inner .bloom-sig-petal {
  --bloom-late: clamp(0,
    calc((var(--bloom-open, 1) - 0.35 - var(--bloom-pstart) * 0.04) / 0.6), 1);
  transform: rotate(calc(-10deg * (1 - var(--bloom-late))))
             scale(calc(0.36 + 0.64 * var(--bloom-late)));
}
```

The line-art here is *fully pre-drawn* — this section is about OPENING,
not drawing (that was the hero's job). Every petal path exists in the DOM
from first paint; only its `transform` and `opacity` change, all
composite-level, no layout. The lerp (0.14/frame, half-life ~5 frames)
makes it scrubbable without jitter: scroll fast, it opens fast; scroll
slow, it opens slow; scroll back up and it closes again. A blush wash
disc behind fills (opacity) as it opens, and a hairline progress bar at
the foot of the pinned view tracks `--bloom-open` literally.

The a11y contract: the copy beside the flower ("Scroll, and the house
scent opens — petal by petal") is plain always-visible text, so the
meaning never depends on the motion. The fallback `var(--bloom-open, 1)`
means no-JS visitors see a fully open flower; under reduced motion the
whole section collapses to `height: auto; position: relative` so nothing
is gated behind scroll.

## Motion math

- **Draw lerp 0.12/frame** (60fps-normalized): half-life ≈ 5.4 frames
  ≈ 90ms. Responsive enough that scroll feels like drawing, not
  scrubbing; slow enough that the stem doesn't snap into existence.
- **Open lerp 0.14/frame**: half-life ≈ 4.6 frames ≈ 77ms. A touch
  quicker than the draw, because opening a flower should feel a shade
  more eager than drawing a stem.
- **Wash settle 1700ms cubic-out**: the displacement collapses along a
  `1 − (1−p)³` curve, so most of the bloom happens in the first 600ms
  and the last 1100ms is the edge finding itself — the slow tail reads
  as *drying*, not *stopping*.
- **Ambient periods 11s / 19s** (line-art sway / sun-breathe) share no
  common divisor — the page never visibly loops, which is most of what
  "alive, not busy" means.
- The reveal ease `cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.8, 0.3, 1)` covers ~65% of its
  travel in the first third of its 900ms duration; with an 18px
  translate the tail settles sub-pixel, so slow reads as calm.

## Performance notes

- One `setProperty` per frame drives ~40 SVG strokes in the hero and ~10
  per scent-note card; the browser batches the style recalc and
  `stroke-dashoffset`/`opacity`/`transform` are all paint/composite
  level — no layout ever runs during drawing.
- `useWashSettle` animates only the `scale` *attribute* of one
  `feDisplacementMap` per wash; the turbulence field is computed once
  by the browser, so each frame is one displacement pass per active
  wash — cheap even at DPR 2.
- The pinned flower is pure CSS transform math on 13 `<g>` elements
  driven by one variable — no rAF per petal, one rAF for the whole
  section, and that loop pauses offscreen (IO) and on hidden tabs.
- All three hook families (draw, wash, open) stop themselves offscreen
  and clean up their observers and listeners on unmount.
- The botanical paths are authored once at module load (no runtime
  cost) and shared by server and client — hydration diffs are
  structurally impossible.

## Accessibility notes

- **Reduced motion:** `usePrefersReducedMotion` (a `useSyncExternalStore`
  over `matchMedia`) disables all three motion hook families; CSS
  fallbacks land everything on a composed still — every line fully
  drawn (`--bloom-draw: 1`), every wash settled (displacement markup
  `scale="0"`), the pinned flower fully open (`--bloom-open: 1`) and the
  pinned section collapsed to auto height so nothing is gated behind
  scroll.
- **No-JS:** the pre-reveal hidden state exists only under `.bloom-js`,
  and every animated value has a finished-state fallback. View-source
  shows a complete, fully-drawn, fully-open page — the SSR frame IS the
  completed page.
- **Semantics:** the botanical SVGs are `aria-hidden` (the titles carry
  the meaning); the pinned section's `<dl>` of scent notes and all copy
  are real semantic content; the CTA and footer links are native
  anchors with a botanical-green `:focus-visible` ring and soft halo.
- **Contrast:** ink 12.4:1, botanical 6.9:1, bark 5.7:1 on cream; sage
  (2.4:1), blush (1.9:1) and gold never label anything — they are
  pigment.
- **Keyboard:** CTA → footer links, in order, each with the focus ring.

## Remix ideas

1. **Tea house.** Swap the perfume premise for a Korean tea house: the
   botanical becomes a tea leaf (camellia), the three scent notes become
   three brews (first/second/third infusion), and the pinned flower
   becomes a single leaf unfurling in hot water — the same scroll-scrub
   but on a vertical leaf-shape rather than a radial flower. Palette:
   cream → hanji, botanical → deeper tea-green.
2. **Ink calligraphy studio.** Drop the watercolor entirely; keep only
   the line-draw and let each stroke carry a calligraphic weight
   variation (variable `stroke-width` along the path via
   `stroke-linecap` and multiple overlaid paths). The pinned moment
   becomes a single 한자 drawing itself stroke-by-stroke in the
   canonical order. Palette: monochrome ink on cream, one seal-red.
3. **Botanical journal (dark).** Invert to a deep botanical-green ground
   (`#1a2418`) with cream line-art — the night-blooming cousin. The
   watercolor washes become bioluminescent (opacity up, displacement
   persistent rather than settling), and the pinned flower becomes a
   moonflower opening only after dark. Distinct from SAKURA by staying
   green-and-cream even inverted, never pink.
