# BOUNCE — breakdown

## The hook

There is a round door on the second floor of a building in Mapo, and
behind it a studio that makes stories for people who are still learning
to tie their shoes. The studio is called 통통 (tong-tong), which is the
sound a rubber ball makes in Korean, and the page is built to make that
sound visible. Everything on it is made of rubber. You do not pick
anything up — you press a button and it squashes wide and short and
springs back with a little overshoot, like it enjoyed being poked.
Headline letters bounce up one at a time. Picture-book cards pop in to
greet you when you scroll down to them. Soft balloons drift toward your
finger. It is a page that likes being touched, and says so by bouncing.

The design bet: a kids' page that is soft without being saccharine, and
springy in a way you can feel through the glass. The bar for "playful" is
that it could so easily be annoying; the way out is to make the physics
real — a spring that overshoots exactly once and settles, the way an
actual rubber stamp would. One boing, not a wobble.

## Art direction

The palette is a box of pastel crayons — butter, cream, sky, peach, grape
— and one deep ink to write with. The rule that keeps it legible is
arithmetic, not taste: every pastel carries the ink at AAA contrast
(8.1:1 to 13.0:1), so a butter button and a grape card and a peach chip
are equally readable. The one color that fails as text — accent orange
`#ff8a5c`, 1.45–2.32:1 on every pastel — was demoted to surface-only:
it is the CTA fill (ink type on it holds 5.59:1) and the spring's blush.
When an accent *word* is needed, plum `#7a4fb8` does it instead
(4.10–5.77:1, AA).

Softness is the texture, full stop. No grain, no scanline, no gradient.
Two things carry the depth instead: rounded everything (radius scales
12px → 28px → 999px), and a soft offset shadow built from two layered
ink-alpha steps (`0 3px 0 rgba(.10)`, `0 10px 22px rgba(.14)`) — a crisp
rubber contact line plus a soft cast, never a blur filter. A 3px ink
outline on every pressable surface gives the squash-and-stretch an edge
to deform against.

Two faces, both round: Fredoka for every Latin glyph, Gaegu for every
Hangul. The display stack is `Fredoka, Gaegu, sans`, so a Korean glyph
inside an English headline falls through into Gaegu with no markup; the
`:lang(ko)` rule pins it, sets weight 700 (Gaegu's hand-drawn strokes
need the bold to read at display size), keeps words whole, and gives
hangul its own line rhythm. The wordmark is 통통 in Gaegu/plum above BOUNCE
in Fredoka/ink — the Korean is the loud line, the English the caption,
the opposite of most bilingual sites, on purpose.

## Technique 1 — spring-press: the rubber snap

Every pressable thing on the page — the two hero buttons, every book
card, the footer mailto — has the same gesture. On press it SQUASHES:
wide and short, `scale(1.12, 0.82)`, in about one frame. On release a
critically-underdamped spring snaps it back through 1 with a small
overshoot, and that single overshoot is the boing.

The spring is integrated with semi-implicit Euler, which is stable where
explicit Euler would explode for a stiff spring:

```ts
// useSpringPress — one axis of the press spring
const a = (-k * (pos - goal) - c * vel) / m;  // Hooke − damping
const nv = vel + a * f;                        // update velocity FIRST
const nx = pos + nv * f;                       // then position
```

The press config (stiffness 380, damping 14, mass 1) is deliberately a
hair under critical damping — critical would settle dead, overdamped
would crawl. Under-damped by just enough to overshoot ~6% once and land.
The press sets `sx=1.12, sy=0.82` (squash wide/short, like a rubber stamp
being pushed), the release sets `sx=1, sy=1`, and the same spring carries
it home, naturally overshooting on the way. `dt` is clamped to 48ms so a
background tab resuming never slingshots the button across the page.

Two structural decisions matter. First, the hook writes only
`style.transform` (scale + a small translateY to nest a press-down), so
no layout ever happens in the hot path — a full mashing of the button is
zero re-renders. Second, there is a CSS fallback for the paths the JS
spring does not cover: keyboard `:active` (the spring is pointer-driven)
and the entire reduced-motion path both get a static `scale(1.08, 0.86)`
press-down with no oscillation, defined in `styles.css`. Keyboard users
get a defined instant response; reduced-motion users get a page that
never moves on its own.

This is the explicit, documented opposite of BLUNT's `spring-press`
honoring. There, the tag is served with anti-easing — `0ms` in, `90ms
linear` out — because a brutalist rubber stamp hits at full speed and
lifts almost as fast. Here the tag is served with a real spring, because
the whole premise of BOUNCE is that things are rubber and rubber
overshoots. Same taxonomy tag, opposite gesture, both honest.

## Technique 2 — char-split-reveal: letters that boing in

The hero wordmark and the footer sign-off split into per-glyph spans
that spring up from below, one at a time, each with its own overshoot.
The split is accessible: the container carries `aria-label` with the
full string, the animated spans are `aria-hidden`, so the headline is
announced once, cleanly.

```ts
// useBounceReveal — per-glyph underdamped spring, staggered 55ms
spans.forEach((span, idx) => {
  g.triggered = true;
  g.startAt = now + idx * STAGGER_MS;   // each glyph waits its turn
});
// in tick: spring translateY(34 → 0) + scale(0.5 → 1) per glyph
```

The glyph config (320 / 12 / 1) is snappier and more underdamped than
the button press — headlines should land loud. Each glyph starts at
`translateY(34px) scale(0.5)` (just below its slot, half size) and
springs to rest. Visibility is gated by a class the hook adds the
instant a glyph's cue arrives (`bounce-glyph--in`), so a glyph never
flashes at rest before its bounce — it flips visible already in its
start position, then springs up. The pre-reveal hidden state itself is
gated behind `.bounce-js`, so without JavaScript the headline is simply
there, complete.

The same hook drives the book cards with a softer preset (220 / 18 / 1):
each card springs `translateY(30px) + scale(0.92)` to rest, and cards
arriving in the same observer batch fan out 90ms apart via a shared
counter. One IntersectionObserver handles both text and cards; the rAF
loop self-terminates when every target has settled.

## Technique 3 — pointer-parallax: balloons on a string

A field of pastel SVG shapes — blobs, chunky stars, clouds, dots — sits
behind the content in three depth layers and drifts toward the pointer.
The hook drives `--bounce-px` / `--bounce-py` (each −1…1) on the field
root; CSS multiplies them by a per-layer depth factor (0.04 far, 0.18
near) so nearer shapes follow more and far ones barely.

```ts
// usePointerParallax — lerp the pointer offset, normalized to 60fps
const k = DRIFT_LERP * (dt / 16.7);     // 0.06/frame
x += (tx - x) * k;  y += (ty - y) * k;
el.style.setProperty("--bounce-px", x.toFixed(4));
```

The lerp is gentle on purpose — the shapes feel like they are catching
up, not tracking. The whole field is `aria-hidden` and
`pointer-events: none`; it is decoration that responds. Two guards keep
it correct on other devices: it runs only when
`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` matches, so on touch the shapes
simply bob; and the rAF loop pauses offscreen (IntersectionObserver) and
when the tab is hidden. Under reduced motion the hook never starts and a
CSS block pins every layer's transform to `none`.

Even without a pointer the field is alive: every shape bobs ±6px on a
mutually-prime period (4.6s to 9.8s), so the periods never sync into a
visible loop. That idle bob is the only ambient motion on the page aside
from the pointer drift — and it dies under reduced motion too.

## The distinction from STICKER (family cap)

STICKER, the 1st playful-pop entry, is **grabbable**: you seize an
object and fling it, with inertia, torque, and restitution off the
edges. The interaction is *you act on the world*. BOUNCE is **reactive**:
you never drag a thing. You press a button and it springs back at you.
You scroll to a card and it bounces in to say hi. You move your finger
and the background drifts toward it. The three techniques here
(`spring-press`, `char-split-reveal`, `pointer-parallax`) are all
reactions — none of them capture a pointer, none move an object you are
holding. Same family (soft, playful, springy motion, Korean-first),
opposite posture: STICKER is a hand grabbing; BOUNCE is a rubber world
bouncing back at you.

## Motion math

- **Press spring 380 / 14 / 1.** Natural frequency ω₀ = √(k/m) ≈ 19.5
  rad/s; damping ratio ζ = c/(2√(km)) ≈ 0.72 — under critical (1.0) so it
  overshoots once, well over 0.0 so it does not oscillate. Settles to
  within 1% in ~520ms. The boing, not the wobble.
- **Char spring 320 / 12 / 1.** ζ ≈ 0.54 — more underdamped, lands louder,
  ~18% overshoot, settles ~480ms. Snappier stiffness so glyphs arrive
  with the snare-drum feel of a kid's storybook.
- **Card spring 220 / 18 / 1.** ζ ≈ 0.92 — just under critical, barely
  overshoots, the softest of the three. Cards settle gently; the bounce
  is in the entrance, not the landing.
- **Parallax lerp 0.06/frame.** Half-life ~11 frames (~185ms) — the
  shapes trail the pointer by a beat, which reads as weight rather than
  stickiness.
- **Bob ±6px on mutually-prime 4.6–9.8s.** The least common multiple of
  the periods is huge, so the field never visibly repeats.

## Performance notes

- Two rAF loops total (parallax drift, bounce-reveal), both
  self-terminating: parallax stops via IntersectionObserver + visibility,
  the reveal loop stops when every glyph and card has settled. Idle page
  = zero frames.
- Everything animated is `transform` (scale, translate) plus `opacity`
  for the reveal gate; no layout or paint properties move. The shadow
  stack, outlines, and pastel fills are rasterized once.
- The reveal hook renders the headline shells once and splits text
  imperatively into the existing DOM — a 14-glyph headline causes zero
  re-renders after the split.
- `dt` is clamped to 48ms in every loop, so a background tab resuming
  never teleports a glyph or slingshots a button.
- No canvas, no images, no dependencies; the heaviest assets are two
  Google font families.

## Accessibility notes

- **Reduced motion:** `usePrefersReducedMotion` (a `useSyncExternalStore`
  over `matchMedia`) disables the parallax entirely, and a CSS block pins
  every shape layer to `transform: none`, kills the idle bob, shows every
  glyph and card at rest, and reduces button/card presses to a 60ms linear
  nudge with no oscillation. The page loses only the boing; nothing is
  hidden.
- **No-JS:** pre-reveal hidden states (glyphs, cards waiting below) are
  gated behind `.bounce-js`; without it, the headline is intact, the
  cards are at rest, the buttons still work as plain links. The no-JS
  page is a finished picture book.
- **Keyboard:** every button is a real link/button; cards are focusable
  `<li>` with `tabindex="0"`. A 3px plum focus ring (cream on the plum
  footer) is the art-directed `:focus-visible`. The CSS `:active` rule
  gives keyboard activation the same static press-down.
- **Split text without double-speak:** the bouncing glyphs are
  `aria-hidden` spans; the container's `aria-label` carries the full
  headline, so the screen reader announces "통통 튀는 이야기" once, not
  glyph by glyph.
- **Contrast:** ink 8.1–13.0:1 on every pastel (AAA body), ink-soft
  4.95:1+ (AA body), plum 4.10–5.77:1 (AA body except 3.60:1 large-only
  on grape), white on the plum footer 5.77:1 (AA body). Accent orange
  exists only as surfaces and shapes, never text.
- **Touch:** pointer-parallax is fine-pointer only; touch devices get
  the idle bob and the springs, never a broken parallax. Nothing
  essential lives behind hover.

## Remix ideas

1. **A kids' recipe site.** Same springs, swap the cards for snacks
   (호떡, 붕어빵, 꿀떡) that bounce in, and the CTA becomes "한 입 먹어보기
   · take a bite" — pressing it squashes the snack and releases a crumb
   burst (SVG, sprung).
2. **A kindergarten enrollment page.** The footer sign-off becomes
   "함께 뛸까요? · shall we jump together?" and the book grid becomes the
   teachers, each card bouncing in to introduce itself. The plum footer
   holds the session times.
3. **A lullaby player.** Drop the shelf, keep the field and the springs;
   each track is a pill button that squashes on press and the floating
   shapes drift to the music's tempo. Reduced motion freezes the field
   into a composed mobile.
